How The Studio works

It is a web page that does all the work on your own device. Open it on your iPhone or your laptop, drop in a Reel, and the listening, the captions, the sound and the export all happen inside your browser. Your video is never sent to a server.

Where does it run?
In the browser of whichever device you open it on. The website works in Safari on your iPhone 17 and in Chrome or Edge on your laptop. The laptop also has its own offline copy.
Where is it hosted?
On GitHub Pages, built from your GitHub repository. It is not hosted on Google Drive; Drive only keeps a note with the links.
What does it cost?
Nothing. No account, no subscription, no limits on length or exports.

The app loads into your browser

GitHub Pages, or the little server in the laptop copy, sends the app: its code, its fonts, the 47 templates and the list of sounds. This is the only thing that comes from the internet each time, and only in one direction, down to you.

Inside your browser, on your device

Nine steps. Two of them are yours, the rest happen by themselves in a minute or two.

  1. You pick your video You

    The browser reads the file straight from your phone or laptop. A copy is kept in this browser so the project opens again later.

  2. It listens

    The sound is pulled out and turned into the simple form speech models read: one channel, 16,000 samples a second.

  3. It writes the words

    Whisper, OpenAI's open speech-recognition model, turns the speech into words, each with its own start and end time. The laptop uses the Medium model (the most accurate, and the best at Hindi to English), the phone uses Base. Speak Hindi or Hinglish and tick Write the captions in English: Whisper translates as it listens, so the English words still follow your voice.

    Run by Transformers.js and ONNX Runtime Web. The model downloads once from Hugging Face, then stays in the browser.

  4. It lines up the timing

    Every word lights up 0.08 seconds before it is spoken, so your eye arrives at the same moment as the voice.

  5. It picks the words that matter

    One punch word per phrase, plus names and numbers, become stand-out words. A caption without one lifts its strongest word, never a little word like "the".

  6. It cuts the captions

    Each gap between words gets a score: a sentence end is best, then a comma, then a pause, then the gap before "and", "but" or a preposition. It never cuts after "a", "the", "to" or "my". The template then sets how many words show at once.

  7. You style and fix You

    A template is a set of fonts, colours and motion drawn over the video live. Change the template or its colour preset, fix any word in the Transcript, move a stand-out, and turn on the Reels safe-zone guide. Changed one caption? Apply to all copies it to every caption. Like the result? Save as my template and new projects start with it.

  8. It places the sound

    Open the Sounds tab. It measures how loud your voice is, finds your pauses and the cuts in the video, and reads the mood of what you say. Then it places sound effects where they have a job: a whoosh on a pause before a new point, a click on "first" and "second", a soft chime on good news, a ding on "follow". A music bed that suits the mood plays under everything, dips while you talk and rises a little in the gaps. Pick Subtle or Punchy, swap or mute any sound, and hear it all with the preview.

    Loudness is measured the broadcast way (ITU-R BS.1770). Mood comes from VADER, a word-based sentiment model, running in the browser. The sounds are free to use: CC0 and public domain.

  9. It exports

    Frame by frame, the video and the caption layer are drawn together, encoded as H.264 and packed into an MP4. Your original audio is copied across untouched. The sound track saves separately, as a short video for Edits or a WAV file, so you can set its level against your voice yourself.

    WebCodecs does the encoding, Mediabunny builds the MP4. Quality is Best by default on both laptop and phone, about 30 Mbps at 1080p. A video smaller than 1080 × 1920 is exported at Instagram size, so the captions stay sharp.

Comes in

  • The app, each time you open it

Goes out

Nothing

No uploads and no account. Your video, words and edits stay on the device.

An MP4 on your device

On the laptop it goes straight into the folder you picked once (Google Drive, The Studio, 05 Finished Reels), named with the date and the project name. On the iPhone, tap Save to Drive and pick Drive, or Save to Files to keep it on the phone.

Edits, then Instagram

In Instagram's Edits app, add the captioned MP4, go to the very start, then Audio, Import, and pick the sound-track video. Post it as a Reel. In Instagram's settings, turn on "Upload at highest quality" so the captions stay sharp.

The science behind it

Most people watch Reels with the sound off, so the captions have to carry the whole Reel. Every template follows the same reading rules.

I know what
you're thinking

Every word has its own time

0.0 s0.5 s1.0 s1.5 s2.0 s

The black line is the voice. Each word lights up 0.08 s before the voice reaches it, and "thinking", the stand-out word, gets the serif and the gold.

  • 2 to 5

    Words per caption on fast Reels

    People read captions at about 160 to 180 words a minute. Energetic templates show 2 to 5 words, calm ones 4 to 8, trailer-style ones 1 to 3. Never more than two lines.

  • 3.7–5.4%

    Text height, as a share of the frame

    Big enough to read at arm's length on a phone, small enough to leave your face clear. No thin or light weights, which break up on compressed video.

  • 7 : 1

    Contrast for text on video

    Text sitting on the video gets a soft dark edge so it keeps at least 7 to 1 contrast, the strictest web accessibility level. Text on a plate needs 4.5 to 1. A checker tests every colour pair in all 47 templates.

  • 1

    Stand-out word per caption

    One place for the eye to land. Two highlights compete, and the viewer reads neither.

  • 0.12–0.6 s

    How long motion lasts

    Fast enough to keep pace with speech, slow enough to be seen. Shorter reads as flicker; longer lags behind the voice.

  • 0.08 s

    Timing lead

    Words that appear slightly early feel in sync; words that appear late feel wrong. Checked against real voice starts: within 0.08 s either way.

  • 15–18 LU

    Music under the voice

    Listeners need speech at least 10 LU above the music to follow it easily, and most people prefer more. The music bed sits 15 (Punchy) to 18 (Subtle) LU under your voice while you talk.

  • −7 to −24 dB

    Sound effects under the voice

    Every effect is set against your measured voice and never goes above it. A big hit sits about 7 to 12 dB under, a pop or whoosh 11 to 15 dB under, a typing texture 20 to 24 dB under, and 2 to 3 dB lower again when it lands on your words.

  • 45 / 125 ms

    How far a sound can drift

    A sound more than 45 ms early or 125 ms late is noticed. Whooshes are timed so their peak lands on the moment, not their start.

  • Bottom third

    Kept clear for Instagram

    Instagram covers roughly the bottom third with your caption and buttons, a strip at the top, and the right edge with likes and shares. Captions sit in the space between.
